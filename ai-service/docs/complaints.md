# Eligibility

A tenant needs an Active lease for the property to raise a lease-related complaint through the normal workflow. Complaints are associated with the tenant, lease, and property so that the appropriate owner can respond. A general question about a property before renting is not a complaint under an active lease.

# Creating a complaint

Open Complaints from the tenant dashboard and choose Raise a Complaint. Select the relevant lease or property where prompted, choose the issue type and priority, and describe the problem clearly. Supported types include Maintenance, Billing, Noise, Safety, Cleanliness, and Other. Priorities include Low, Medium, High, and Critical.

Use Critical only for issues that genuinely require the highest attention. EstateSync complaint tracking is not a replacement for emergency services. For immediate danger or a medical, fire, or public-safety emergency, contact the appropriate local emergency service first.

# Description and attachments

Explain what happened, where it occurs, when it began, and what outcome is needed. Attach relevant photos if they help the owner understand the issue. Do not include passwords, identity-document numbers, payment-card information, private messages unrelated to the issue, or images exposing sensitive documents. Uploading an attachment does not itself change complaint priority or status.

# Complaint statuses

Open means the complaint has been submitted and awaits or has begun attention. In Progress means the owner is working on it. Resolved means the owner marked the issue as addressed. Closed means the tenant confirmed completion or the workflow reached its final closed state. Status changes are recorded through authorized actions and produce relevant notifications.

# Owner handling

The owner views complaints connected to owned properties. Open the record, review its type, priority, description, and permitted attachments, then update status as work proceeds. Move it to In Progress when actively addressing it and to Resolved when the proposed resolution is complete. Do not mark an unresolved safety or maintenance issue resolved merely to remove it from an active list.

# Tenant closure

When the owner marks a complaint Resolved, the tenant reviews the result. If satisfied, the tenant can Close it. If the issue persists and the interface provides a supported next action, follow that process or contact support. Creating repeated duplicate complaints can make history harder to follow; update or reference the existing complaint when appropriate.

# Common blocked actions

Raise Complaint may be unavailable when there is no Active lease for the selected property. An owner cannot manage a complaint belonging to another owner’s property. A tenant cannot perform owner status transitions. Closed complaints cannot be processed as if they were new Open complaints. Refresh before updating a complaint opened in an older browser tab.

# Billing complaints

A Billing complaint records an issue for review but does not automatically cancel, refund, or mark a bill Paid. Continue to inspect the actual bill and payment status. Do not share complete payment credentials in the complaint. Use transaction or bill references that the application safely displays.

# Privacy

Complaint information is visible only through authorized tenant, owner, and staff processes. Attachments should be limited to evidence needed for the issue. The assistant can explain complaint workflow but cannot inspect private complaint content or reveal another user’s complaint.
